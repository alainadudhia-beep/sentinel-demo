PROJECT CLOSURE NOTE
Engagement Reference: ENG-016
Date: February 2024
Prepared by: Engagement Manager

CLIENT & MANDATE
Client: Vantage Capital Partners (sell-side PE)
Target: NestWise (smart home hardware + subscription services, UK)
Engagement type: Vendor Due Diligence
Sector: Consumer Technology

COMMERCIAL TERMS
Planned duration: 6 weeks
Actual duration: 8 weeks
Planned fee: £215k
Actual fee: £265k
Team: 1 Partner, 1 Manager, 3 Consultants, 2 Analysts

SCOPE
Smart home market dynamics, NestWise hardware + software bundle positioning, subscription revenue quality (attach rate, churn by cohort, device-service linkage), retail channel vs DTC analysis, international opportunity (DACH, Nordics), platform/ecosystem competitive threat (Amazon, Google, Apple).

This was a complex scope and in retrospect should have been 7 weeks from the outset.

Scope changes:
- Week 2: Vantage asked for a section on B2B/commercial property installation as a growth vector (not in IM, surfaced by management). Added as a new workstream, £20k uplift.
- Week 4: Following initial bidder engagement, Vantage requested a detailed cohort-level profitability analysis (hardware + software margin by cohort). Required bespoke data build. £15k uplift.
- Week 6: A US strategic bidder asked for an assessment of NestWise's patent portfolio's commercial relevance. Outside our scope; we provided a framing document only and recommended specialist IP counsel.
- Week 7: Bidder Q&A round generated 47 questions. Team spent a full week on responses.

EXECUTION NOTES
This was a punishing engagement and the overrun was partly foreseeable.

The hardware-software bundling model created an unusual unit economics structure that took the team several days to model correctly. Gross margin analysis was complex — hardware margin (negative or low on first device) subsidised by subscription attach; traditional SaaS metrics didn't directly apply. The Manager built a bespoke profitability model that eventually worked well but consumed significant time.

Subscription data quality was good (Stripe + bespoke internal analytics), but the device-level data (which cohort of devices had which subscription states) was in a separate system (a proprietary IoT backend) and required an API data pull. NestWise's engineering team were helpful but slow — each data request took 2-3 days to turn around. This became a bottleneck in weeks 3 and 4.

Client behaviour: Vantage were broadly good clients but increasingly anxious as the process extended. In weeks 6-7 they became more directive — specifying how conclusions should be framed in certain sections. We pushed back on two occasions where the requested framing was inconsistent with the evidence. These conversations were uncomfortable but necessary.

NestWise management were confident, technically sophisticated, and — in two instances — obstructive when questions touched on platform dependency risk (the Amazon Alexa and Google Home integrations accounted for 38% of device activations; if either removed integration this would be material). They answered questions about platform risk with prepared talking points rather than substantive analysis.

Consultant turnover: one consultant left the firm at the end of week 5, mid-engagement. Their replacement needed a week to ramp.

Expert calls: 10 completed. Platform ecosystem specialists, smart home retail buyers, ex-Nest commercial lead.

OUTCOME
Sale process ongoing at time of writing. VDD report delivered. Multiple bidders in process.

KEY LESSONS
- Hardware + subscription bundle models need a bespoke unit economics framework built in week 1; don't adapt a SaaS framework
- IoT backend data requests have long lead times; identify all required data extracts in day 1 kick-off and submit immediately
- Platform dependency risk in consumer tech is frequently a contentious topic with management; triangulate independently and don't accept talking points
- 47-question bidder Q&A rounds are common in complex tech VDDs; assume one full week for Q&A at end of process and factor into timeline
- Consultant attrition mid-project is an underrated operational risk; always have a named backup resource identified

RISK FLAGS (materialised)
- Data availability (HIGH): IoT backend data requests slow; bottleneck in weeks 3-4.
- Client behaviour (MEDIUM): Vantage directiveness around conclusion framing in late stages.
- Management access (MEDIUM): NestWise management deflected on platform dependency risk questions.
- Team continuity (HIGH): Consultant departure mid-engagement; replacement ramp cost.
- Scope expansion (HIGH): Four distinct scope additions; timeline extended by 2 weeks.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Material overrun (2 weeks, £50k). Root causes: scope underestimated at outset; IoT data access bottleneck; consultant departure. Work product strong. Client relationship intact.
