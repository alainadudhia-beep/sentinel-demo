PROJECT CLOSURE NOTE
Engagement Reference: ENG-020
Date: June 2022
Prepared by: Engagement Manager

CLIENT & MANDATE
Client: Castleton Retail Group (listed, £1.2bn revenue, UK)
Type: Strategy — Board mandate
Engagement type: Strategy
Sector: Retail

COMMERCIAL TERMS
Planned duration: 8 weeks
Actual duration: 11 weeks
Planned fee: £320k
Actual fee: £395k
Team: 1 Partner, 1 Manager, 3 Consultants, 2 Analysts

SCOPE
Omnichannel strategy review: assessment of Castleton's digital/physical channel integration, customer data and loyalty programme effectiveness, store portfolio rationalisation options, and recommendations for a 3-year omnichannel investment roadmap.

Scope changes:
- Week 3: CFO requested a parallel workstream on cost of serving customers by channel — not in original brief. Agreed as a new workstream with separate resource allocation. £45k uplift, partly attributed to a separate workstream budget.
- Week 5: CEO asked for an additional board presentation on "competitor benchmarking deep-dive" following a strategic threat from a new entrant. Added 1 week of work; absorbed.
- Week 7: Board request to extend the investment roadmap to 5 years (was 3 years). Minor extension, no fee change.
- Week 9: A new CEO was appointed mid-engagement (the sitting CEO announced departure during week 6). The incoming CEO asked for a full re-brief and had different strategic priorities. This was the primary driver of the overrun.

EXECUTION NOTES
The CEO transition was the defining event of this engagement and nothing could have anticipated it.

The outgoing CEO had been a strong sponsor of the project and had a clear point of view on the strategic direction. The incoming CEO (internal promotion, previously CFO of a subsidiary) came with different instincts — more operationally focused, more cautious on digital investment. When she re-briefed us in week 9, she indicated the board mandate had shifted and asked us to place more emphasis on store portfolio rationalisation and less on digital transformation.

This required a partial restructure of the recommendation. We maintained the strategic substance but significantly reweighted the emphasis. The Partner managed the CEO transition carefully — a credit to the relationship management throughout.

Separately: this was a large team engagement and coordination overhead was high. Three consultants on a strategy project creates competing analytical approaches that need to be actively managed. The Manager spent more time on team coordination than on direct analysis, which stretched the delivery timeline.

The cost-of-serving analysis (scope change 1) was analytically valuable but created an internal political dynamic at Castleton — the digital team felt it was being used to justify store investment at their expense. We were drawn into stakeholder management that went beyond our normal advisory role.

Expert calls: 8 completed. Omnichannel retail specialists, customer data platform vendors, ex-John Lewis commercial director.

OUTCOME
Strategy delivered and accepted by the board. Investment roadmap approved (modified version with more store rationalisation than originally envisaged). Castleton retained us for a follow-on implementation planning phase.

KEY LESSONS
- CEO transitions during strategy projects are a material risk; build in a contingency assumption for senior stakeholder change in engagements >6 weeks
- Three-consultant strategy projects need active coordination management — appoint a team lead below Manager level
- Cost-of-serving analyses in omnichannel can become politically charged internally; brief client sponsor on this risk upfront
- Board strategy projects frequently expand in scope as they progress — build this into fee and timeline conversations at kick-off

RISK FLAGS (materialised)
- Senior stakeholder change (CRITICAL): CEO transition in week 6; required partial project restructure.
- Internal politics (MEDIUM): Cost-of-serving analysis created internal tension; required stakeholder management.
- Scope expansion (HIGH): Four scope additions; primary driver of 3-week overrun.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Significant overrun but largely attributable to an unforeseeable event (CEO change). Client relationship strong; retained for follow-on work. Work product praised at board level.
