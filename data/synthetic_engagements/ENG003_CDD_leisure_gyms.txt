PROJECT CLOSURE NOTE
Engagement Reference: ENG-003
Date: June 2023
Prepared by: Engagement Manager

CLIENT & MANDATE
Client: Fortis Private Equity
Target: IronGate Fitness (mid-market gym chain, 34 sites, UK)
Engagement type: Commercial Due Diligence
Sector: Leisure

COMMERCIAL TERMS
Planned duration: 3 weeks
Actual duration: 4.5 weeks
Planned fee: £120k
Actual fee: £148k (scope change, agreed uplift)
Team: 1 Partner, 1 Manager, 2 Consultants, 1 Analyst (+ 1 additional analyst from week 3)

SCOPE
Original scope: UK gym market sizing and structural trends (budget vs mid-market dynamics post-COVID), IronGate competitive positioning, member economics (LTV, attrition, cohort analysis by site), and management assessment.

Scope change 1 (week 2): Fortis requested an additional workstream on IronGate's PT (personal training) revenue stream following a site visit that suggested it was more material than the IM had indicated. Added 4 days of work; fee uplift agreed at £18k.

Scope change 2 (week 3): Request to extend geographic analysis to include Scotland (3 sites) which had been excluded from original brief. Absorbed by team — no fee change, but added ~3 days of work, contributing to timeline extension.

EXECUTION NOTES
The engagement was harder than it looked at the outset. A few issues compounded:

First, IronGate's membership data was held across two legacy systems (a Mindbody instance and a bespoke database from a pre-2019 acquisition) and the reconciliation took significantly longer than anticipated. The CFO was cooperative but not technically capable of helping us bridge the two datasets — we ended up doing it ourselves in week 2, which ate into the competitive analysis time.

Second, the PT revenue workstream (scope change 1) revealed a more complex picture than expected — PT income was partly off-platform and the accounting treatment was inconsistent across sites. This flagged a potential quality of earnings issue that Fortis needed to understand, which generated a round of follow-up questions that ran into week 4.

Client communication was fine but Fortis's deal team was under their own internal pressure from a fund deployment timeline and pushed for the final report 4 days before the revised deadline. We delivered a draft but it required a significant round of revisions based on their feedback, which is unusual for a CDD — the feedback felt like scope creep disguised as review comments.

Expert calls: 9 completed (gym operators, a fitness industry analyst, ex-Nuffield senior commercial lead, two PTs who had worked at IronGate sites).

OUTCOME
Deal completed after an extended period. Recommended proceed with conditions. The PT revenue quality of earnings issue was flagged prominently and Fortis used it to negotiate a price chip. Member attrition data was concerning in the 18-24 cohort (likely pandemic distortion plus competitive pressure from budget operators).

KEY LESSONS
- Leisure assets with multi-site member data are a data integration challenge first, analysis challenge second. Always ask about system landscape before scoping
- PT/ancillary revenue at gym chains is frequently messy; treat as a separate workstream by default, don't bundle with main member economics
- "Review comments" that constitute new analysis requests should be pushed back on formally — name it as a scope change
- Post-COVID cohort data for gyms is still distorted; be explicit with clients about which periods are comparable

RISK FLAGS (materialised)
- Data quality (HIGH): dual-system membership data required significant reconciliation effort. Extended timeline.
- Scope creep (MEDIUM): client review comments constituted additional analysis requests. Partially absorbed, partially charged.
- Quality of earnings (MEDIUM): PT revenue accounting inconsistency surfaced mid-project; required follow-up workstream.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Difficult but successful engagement. Overran on both time and fee, though the fee overrun was partially captured through the agreed scope change uplift. Work product was strong. Fortis relationship intact.
