PROJECT CLOSURE NOTE
Engagement Reference: ENG-021
Date: October 2023
Prepared by: Engagement Manager

CLIENT & MANDATE
Client: Dalton Consumer Brands (private, £180m revenue, UK/EU)
Type: Board mandate — new market entry
Engagement type: Strategy
Sector: FMCG

COMMERCIAL TERMS
Planned duration: 6 weeks
Actual duration: 6 weeks
Planned fee: £245k
Actual fee: £245k
Team: 1 Partner, 1 Manager, 2 Consultants, 1 Analyst

SCOPE
Assessment of US market entry opportunity for Dalton's core personal care product range. Scope: US personal care market sizing (relevant segments), retail channel dynamics (mass, drug, specialty, DTC), competitor analysis, route-to-market options (own subsidiary, distributor, JV, licence), and a go/no-go recommendation with 3-year revenue model.

Scope changes (week 3): Client asked us to include a section on Canada as a lower-risk "stepping stone" market given concerns arising from the US analysis. Absorbed within existing team capacity.

EXECUTION NOTES
Well-run engagement with one notable dynamic.

The US market analysis was analytically strong. The team had good cross-firm experience with US consumer markets and we were able to quickly build a credible market sizing and competitive picture. The channel dynamics analysis was particularly good — we identified that Dalton's price point positioned it awkwardly against both mass (too premium) and specialty (not premium enough), which was the key strategic insight.

Client dynamic: Dalton's CEO was entrepreneurially confident and came into the engagement with a strong existing view that the US was the right market and that direct DTC was the right channel. Our analysis pointed in a different direction — Canada first, then US, with a distributor model initially. The CEO was initially resistant and pushed us to "make the case" for the US/DTC route. The Partner managed this well — we presented both scenarios with evidence and let the board decide, rather than being pushed into advocacy.

Board presentation (week 6): attended by 4 board members. Two were strongly in favour of the CEO's US/DTC view; two were more cautious. The Partner presented findings in a structured way that allowed the board to reach a genuinely informed conclusion. The board voted for the Canada-first approach, which surprised the CEO.

Canada section: well-received and probably validated the scope addition.

Expert calls: 7 completed. US retail buyers (Target, Walgreens), ex-Unilever North America, DTC brand founders, Canadian distributor.

OUTCOME
Strategy delivered and accepted (Canada-first decision). Client is now in the scoping phase for a Canada pilot. Good outcome, good process.

KEY LESSONS
- FMCG market entry mandates often start with a strong management hypothesis — prepare to challenge it with evidence, not to support it
- Price positioning analysis is often the most important insight in market entry work; spend time on it in week 1
- Board-level strategy work requires the Partner to be "honest broker" when management and board have different instincts — this is a relationship management skill as much as an analytical one
- Canada as an entry route to North America is underused as a strategic option; include by default in US entry assessments

RISK FLAGS (materialised)
- Client pressure (MEDIUM): CEO pushed for conclusions to support existing hypothesis. Managed by Partner.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Clean delivery. Strong analytical work product. Client relationship strong. Good outcome.
