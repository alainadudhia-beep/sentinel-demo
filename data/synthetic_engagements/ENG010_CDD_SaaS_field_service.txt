CLIENT & MANDATE
Client: Summit Growth Partners
Target: FieldOps (field service management SaaS, UK mid-market, plumbing/electrical/HVAC verticals)
Engagement type: Commercial Due Diligence
Sector: Technology / SaaS

COMMERCIAL TERMS
Planned duration: 3 weeks
Actual duration: 3 weeks
Planned fee: £125k
Actual fee: £125k
Team: 1 Partner, 1 Manager, 2 Consultants, 1 Analyst

SCOPE
UK field service management software market (TAM, competitive landscape, vertical-specific dynamics), FieldOps product positioning vs Jobber, ServiceTitan, Commusoft, customer cohort analysis (ARR, churn, NRR), ICP definition and go-to-market assessment.

Scope addition: Summit asked for a view on FieldOps's international expansion optionality (Ireland + Australia) in week 2. Not in original brief but limited desk research + two expert calls gave us enough to provide a directional view. Absorbed within existing team capacity.

EXECUTION NOTES
Well-run engagement. FieldOps had clean Salesforce data and a founder-led finance function that had clearly prepared carefully for the DD process. ARR bridge from year 1 to current was clean and reconcilable.

One interesting dynamic: FieldOps was simultaneously in conversations with two other potential acquirers, which meant the management team were managing multiple DD processes simultaneously. They were polished but occasionally slipped into "mode-switching" — answering questions in ways that felt calibrated for a different buyer profile (likely a trade buyer). Worth flagging for Summit's awareness.

Churn analysis was the key analytical challenge. FieldOps had good logo retention (92%) but when we ran dollar retention it was lower (104% NRR vs the 118% in the IM). The difference was driven by a large cohort of SMB customers who had downgraded rather than churned — the IM presentation had obscured this by combining upgrades and new ARR.

Expert calls: 6 completed. Strong contacts in the trades software space.

OUTCOME
Deal proceeded. Recommended proceed with revised NRR assumption. Summit incorporated the revised NRR into their model and it affected the price paid. Good outcome for the client.

KEY LESSONS
- Multi-process management teams need to be understood as such — their answers are calibrated for a composite audience. Push for specificity.
- ARR bridge analysis should always reconcile to a dollar retention calculation, not just a logo retention figure
- IM presentations of NRR sometimes obscure downgrade cohorts — rebuild from transactional data, don't accept the summary

RISK FLAGS (materialised)
- Deal process (LOW): Target running parallel process; management behaviour slightly calibrated away from buy-side framing.
- Revenue quality (MEDIUM): NRR in IM overstated due to downgrade treatment. Corrected in analysis.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Clean delivery. Strong work product with a material finding (NRR revision) that provided direct value to the client. Good engagement.
