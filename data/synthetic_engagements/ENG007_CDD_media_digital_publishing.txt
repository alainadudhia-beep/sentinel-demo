PROJECT CLOSURE NOTE
Engagement Reference: ENG-007
Date: April 2023
Prepared by: Engagement Manager

CLIENT & MANDATE
Client: Watershed Capital
Target: The Brief (digital newsletter + events business, B2B professional audience)
Engagement type: Commercial Due Diligence
Sector: Media

COMMERCIAL TERMS
Planned duration: 2 weeks
Actual duration: 2 weeks
Planned fee: £90k
Actual fee: £90k
Team: 1 Partner, 1 Manager, 1 Consultant, 1 Analyst

SCOPE
Digital B2B media market assessment, subscriber economics (paid newsletter benchmarking), advertising revenue quality, events contribution margin assessment, competitive positioning vs sector newsletters and broader professional media landscape.

Scope addition: Watershed asked for a view on the sponsorship revenue model as a third revenue stream — not in the original brief. Team absorbed this in the context of the advertising assessment; it didn't materially extend the work.

EXECUTION NOTES
Tight but well-managed. The Brief was a smaller asset and Watershed knew it — they'd sized the work appropriately for a 2-week sprint.

Subscriber data from Substack was clean and easy to work with. Advertising and sponsorship revenue was less structured — it lived in a combination of spreadsheets and the founder's email threads. Getting clean pipeline data took two days of back-and-forth. The founder was enthusiastic and transparent but not commercially sophisticated; some of the revenue projections in the IM were optimistic in ways that weren't immediately obvious.

Events business: The Brief ran 4 events per year. P&L was available but cost allocation was inconsistent (some overheads recharged, some not). We normalised and found contribution margin was materially lower than presented — this became a significant finding.

Expert calls: 4 completed. Limited pool for specialist B2B newsletters; used broader digital media contacts.

OUTCOME
Deal proceeded. Recommended proceed with revised revenue model — our normalised events margin was lower than the IM suggested, and we revised the advertising growth assumption down. Watershed incorporated both into their model.

KEY LESSONS
- Founder-led media businesses often have revenue in email threads and informal agreements — build in discovery time for commercial data
- Events P&L in media businesses frequently has inconsistent cost allocation; treat it as a standalone rebuild
- 2-week CDDs on smaller media assets are achievable; keep team lean

RISK FLAGS (materialised)
- Data quality (LOW-MEDIUM): Advertising/sponsorship data unstructured; required consolidation.
- Revenue quality (MEDIUM): Events margin overstated in IM due to inconsistent cost allocation. Surfaced and flagged.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Good engagement. Clean delivery within timeline. Watershed were pleased with the events margin finding — it was actionable for them.
